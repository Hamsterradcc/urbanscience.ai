Research agenda

Five directions, one question each.

Each direction is framed by a question, the data it draws on, and the methods it uses. Outputs are released through the platforms rather than listed here.

01 Representation 02 Perception 03 Mobility 04 Climate 05 Agents
How directions are used

Directions are the lab's organising categories. Data and indicators go to UrbanProbe; models, agents, and benchmarks go to XUrbanAI; open collaboration runs through the City Computing Collective.

01 · Representation

Urban representation & multimodal learning

How can cities be represented from imagery, maps, mobility, and text in ways that support valid comparison across places?

Representations are the layer every downstream measure depends on. The direction asks what a model has actually learned about a city, and whether that transfers.

Questions
What urban features do multimodal models encode, and how do their representations differ between cities?
Data
Street-level and satellite imagery, basemaps, mobility traces, and urban text corpora.
Methods
Multimodal learning, spatial embeddings, and interpretability analysis of learned features.
02 · Perception

Human-centered sensing & perception

How do people perceive and experience urban environments, and how can perception be measured at scale without losing what makes it subjective?

Perception links the physical environment to behaviour. The direction treats perception as a measurement problem with validity, bias, and heterogeneity to account for.

Questions
Which environmental features shape perceived safety, comfort, and vitality, and for whom?
Data
Surveys and ratings, crowdsourced annotations, environmental sensors, and imagery.
Methods
Perception models, measurement validation, and mixed-method designs.
03 · Mobility

Mobility, accessibility & inequality

Who can reach what, at what cost, and how do urban form and infrastructure shape those differences?

Access is where urban form becomes opportunity or exclusion. The direction measures it across modes and groups, and tests which interventions change it.

Questions
How do street environments and networks affect route choice, mode choice, and access to services?
Data
Transit and road networks, mobility and trip data, census and amenity records.
Methods
Accessibility measures, spatial econometrics, and causal research designs.
04 · Climate

Climate resilience & environmental justice

How are heat, air pollution, flooding, and green space distributed across a city, and which communities bear the exposure?

Environmental risk is unevenly distributed and often compounds social disadvantage. The direction maps exposure, links it to outcomes, and evaluates responses.

Questions
Where do exposure, vulnerability, and adaptive capacity overlap, and what reduces the gap?
Data
Remote sensing, environmental monitoring, land cover, and health and demographic records.
Methods
Exposure modelling, spatial statistics, and equity-focused evaluation.
05 · Agents

Urban agents, simulation & decision support

When can computational agents and simulations support planning decisions, and how should their claims be tested before anyone relies on them?

Agents and simulations promise cheaper evidence. The direction treats that promise as a hypothesis, with evaluation protocols that test structure, not only averages.

Questions
Do simulated populations preserve the social and spatial structure of real ones, and where do they fail?
Data
Scenario inputs, administrative records, survey experiments, and planning documents.
Methods
Agent-based simulation, evaluation of language and multimodal models, and decision-support design.
Outputs

Released through the platforms.
